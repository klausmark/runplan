# Evidence base for the first 10K generator

This document records the peer-reviewed sources that underpin the deterministic
first 10K generator in `runplan.generation`. Every rule in the algorithm cites
the source that motivated it. The catalogue is intentionally short and uses
only sources indexed in PubMed or authoritative sports-science reviews.

The generator is a coaching tool, not a medical device. The citations justify
the periodic structure, the intensity distribution, and the safety limits; they
are not personal medical advice.

## Core principles

### 1. The pyramidal intensity distribution

The 92-plan analysis by Knopp et al. (2024, Sports Medicine Open; PMID
38695978) is the strongest available evidence for how recreational distance
runners actually distribute training intensity. Across 92 twelve-week marathon
plans, the average intensity distribution was:

| Volume band | Z1 | Z2 | Z3 | Z4 | Z5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| High (>90 km/week) | 15% | 67% | 10% | 5% | 3% |
| Middle (65-90 km/week) | 14% | 63% | 18% | 2% | 3% |
| Low (<65 km/week) | 12% | 67% | 17% | 2% | 2% |

The 10K generator aims for a similar distribution at a smaller total volume:
most running in Zone 2, a meaningful Zone 3 share, and a small Zone 4-5 share.

### 2. Polarized training for endurance

Hydren and Cohen (2015, Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research; PMID
26595137) reviewed the polarized cardiovascular endurance model. They report
that nearly all training time is spent at light intensity or very hard
intensity, with very limited time at threshold or race pace. The review
finds effect sizes for the polarized model are consistently superior to the
threshold model for highly trained athletes.

The 10K generator applies a *soft* polarization: most volume lives in Zones
1-2, while Zone 3-5 work is concentrated in one quality session per week with
no "moderate" tempo work bleeding into easy days.

### 3. The 80% rule for elite runners

Haugen, Sandbakk, Seiler, and Tønnessen (2022, Sports Medicine - Open; PMID
35362850) summarise training characteristics of world-class distance runners.
About 80% of total running volume is performed at low intensity throughout the
training year. The 10K generator targets a similar 80% in Zone 1-2.

## Progression and recovery

### 4. The 10% rule does not prevent injuries

Buist, Bredeweg, van Mechelen, Lemmink, Pepping, and Diercks (2008, American
Journal of Sports Medicine; PMID 17940147) randomised 532 novice runners to a
graded 13-week program based on the 10% rule against a standard 8-week program.
The graded program did not reduce the running-related injury rate (20.8% versus
20.3%, chi-squared 0.016, P=0.90).

The 10K generator therefore does not centre its progression on a weekly
percentage. It restricts the maximum weekly increase to a profile-dependent
cap, but the primary safety mechanism is the four-week microcycle with a
forced recovery week, the four-week mesocycle, and the long-run share limit.

### 5. Nielsen and colleagues on excessive progression

Nielsen et al. (2013, Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research; PMID
22990565) used GPS to show that excessive progression in weekly volume is
associated with running-related injuries. The 10K generator combines absolute
caps and profile caps so that even cautious runners progress at no more than
5% per week.

### 6. Banister's fitness-fatigue model

The Wikipedia article on sports periodization summarises the Banister
fitness-fatigue model, which the original work (Banister, Calvert, Savage, and
Bach, 1975, Australian Journal of Sports Medicine) defines as the algebraic
sum of a positive fitness component and a negative fatigue component, each
decaying exponentially after a training stimulus. The 10K generator uses this
intuition to schedule a recovery week every fourth week, allowing fitness to
consolidate and fatigue to decay.

## Day spacing and concurrent training

### 7. Concurrent training interference

Wilson et al. (2012, Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, as cited
in the Wikipedia article on sports periodization) ran a meta-analysis on
concurrent training and found that resistance and endurance sessions need at
least six hours of separation, or placement on alternate days, to preserve
both adaptations. The 10K generator treats its own quality and long sessions
as the high-stress items and forbids them on consecutive days.

## Taper

### 8. Taper reduces volume and preserves intensity

Spilsbury, Fudge, Nimmo, and Faulkner (2021, Applied Physiology, Nutrition,
and Metabolism; PMID 34062089) compared a low-volume taper with a race-pace
final interval to a low-volume taper with a faster final interval. Both
groups improved 1500 m performance, with the higher-intensity final session
producing the largest early-race segment gains. The 10K generator's taper
reduces weekly volume by 40-50% in the final two weeks while keeping one short
sharpener session at near-race intensity.

### 9. Taper improves muscular performance

Luden et al. (2010, Journal of Applied Physiology; PMID 20299622) showed a 3%
race performance improvement after a taper phase, with myosin heavy chain IIa
fiber diameter increasing 7%. The 10K generator's taper preserves one short
intensity session per week to maintain neuromuscular readiness.

### 10. Banister on taper predictive models

Banister, Carter, and Zarkadas (1999, European Journal of Applied Physiology;
PMID 10029340) define a training theory that predicts the effectiveness of
different taper formats. The 10K generator uses an exponential taper curve
rather than a linear reduction so the first recovery days are the largest
single drop in load.

## Lactate-guided threshold work

### 11. Modern interval training

Casado, Foster, Bakken, and Tjelta (2023, International Journal of
Environmental Research and Public Health; PMID 36900796) describe the
lactate-guided threshold interval training (LGTIT) model used by world-class
distance runners. Three to four LGTIT sessions plus one VO2max session per
week, on a base of low-intensity volume, characterise the modern approach.
The 10K generator includes a continuous-tempo and a cruise-interval workout
type that approximate the LGTIT pattern at a recreational intensity.

## Energy availability

### 12. Low energy availability

Cupka and Sedliak (2023, European Journal of Translational Myology; PMID
37052052) review low energy availability in male endurance athletes and link
it to reduced recovery, increased injury risk, and decreased performance. The
10K generator does not create its own nutritional advice, but its weekly
volumes are conservative so that the generated plan is achievable with normal
intake.

## Periodisation structure

### 13. Macro, meso, micro cycles

The Wikipedia article on sports periodization defines the macrocycle, the
mesocycle (4-6 microcycles during preparation), and the microcycle (2-3 hard
days followed by recovery). The 10K generator uses a 12-week macrocycle
split into foundation, build, peak, and taper blocks, with four-week
mesocycles and one-week microcycles.

### 14. The minimum effective dose for completion

The Wikipedia article on sports periodization cites Stone et al. (2021,
Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research; PMID 34132223) on the variety
of valid periodization models. This flexibility is why the 10K generator
scales its progression from 8 to 16 weeks and lets the user choose a
progression profile rather than enforcing a single rigid model.

## Bias toward intensity classification

### 15. Race pace is not a training zone

The 10K generator never assigns a structured pace to a race day. Racers should
pick their effort based on conditions, fitness, and pacing plan rather than a
pre-baked numeric target. Pace targets in training are also gated on
user-supplied known pace data, so the generator never invents a pace from a
generic level description.

## Open clinical questions

The 10K generator reflects what current peer-reviewed evidence supports. The
following items remain open in the literature and are not claimed by the
generator:

- Whether a 4-week or a 3-week microcycle is superior for novice runners.
- The optimal minimum long-run share for novices.
- Whether strides after easy runs reduce injury risk.
- The exact taper curve that maximises 10K performance across experience
  levels.

The generator picks a defensible default for each of these and surfaces the
choice in its diagnostics so the user can override it.
